Newton's Laws:



DRAGONS:

A program/game supposedly to teach Newton's Laws (well Laws 1 and 2). You fly a dragon around the sky trying to catch apples. The dragon moves as a mass would move in a frictionless environment.

  1. The dragon accelerates in the direction of the cursor, and maintains this motion, to slow down you must accelerate in the opposite direction.
There is the option of having the dragon fly according to "Aristotilian" or "common sense" mechanics to compare to "Newtonian". Two versions of "common sense" mechanics:
  1. dragon moves at constant speed only as long as the cursor is pressed , it stops dead as soon as the cursor is no longer pressed ("common sense" laws)
  2. dragon accelerates as long as the cursor is pressed, but slows to a stop as soon as the cursor is no longer pressed ("gas-pedal" laws)
I've often thought that with current students' knowledge and experience of motion controlled by cursor keys (in Gameboys, computers etc) it would be an interesting discussion/investigation as to what is the best way to use cursor keys to control movement: should they control position, speed or acceleration? Should they provide constant values or gradually increasing values the longer the cursor is held down or each time the cursor is pressed in the same direction (to decrease value must press in opposite direction)? When cursor is not pressed should you revert to a default setting or keep last setting attained or gradually change from one to other? I think after a detailed look at all the options we'd end up with Newton's Laws as the best means of controlling movement, because those are nature's chosen rules - (perhaps Newton's Laws with friction would actually be the best; I wasn't able to add friction into Dragons; various problems... maybe in the next version...)
I did add gravity though, for when you think the game is getting too easy...
This 'thought' has now been made manifest! See Cursor Car below!

    • CURSOR CAR:

    A program/game that again (like DRAGONS) supposedly leads the user to the relevence of Newton's Laws (well Laws 1 and 2).
    You get to drive a car around a track using the cursor keys
    But what does pressing a cursor actually do? Does it change the car's distance, does it change its velocity, or does it change its acceleration?
    In this program you get to try all three and decide which is most 'realistic'.
    How fast can you get round the track? My best lap time is 6.8 seconds! Beat that!
    And hopefully students will find that cursor changing 'velocity' is the best - Newton's 2nd Law!
