INSTRUCTIONS FOR USING THE 'PHASE' PROGRAM:

The picture shows a snap-shot of a particle vibrating up and down.

By clicking ON the picture you can make the picture jump to the next 'frame' - so making the particle move up and down.

The 'RUN' button makes the picture automatically step through the frames.

In frame 9 (if there was a frame 9...) the particle has returned to the same position as it started, moving in exactly the same direction as it was at the start - so it is exactly as it was in frame 1. Its motion has gone 'around in a circle'; it has returned 'back to how it started'.

So to animate the movement of the particle there is no need for frames 9, 10 etc. we only have to repeat frames 1 to 8 continuously.

The vibration of particles in a wave follow exactly this sort of 'circular cycle' - they move back and forth around a centre point.

The PHASE of any particular point in that cycle is a measure of where the particle is in its repetition of its movement - is it at the start of the vibration? A quarter of the way through? Half way through? Three-quarters of the way through?

And where the particle is in its cycle is expressed using the methods used to describe 'distance' around a circle - ANGLES.

So:

  • At the start = 0 o
  • A quarter of the way through = 90 o
  • Half way through =180 o
  • Three-quarters of the way through = 270 o
  • etc.

Phase mainly becomes important when we think of how different particles are at different stages of the cycle - and is a way of describing how they are at different stages - eg if particle A is out of phase with particle B by 90 o then that means that they are a quarter of a cycle difference in what point of their vibration they are in - if particle A is at the start, particle B would be quarter of the way through; if particle A was at 270 o particle B would be at the start; etc.
